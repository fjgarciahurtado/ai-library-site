## Summary

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra (`gpt-6.1-terra`) on 2026-10-26. It is a mid-priced model
aimed at visual reasoning, computer use, and clear writing. It sits between GPT-6.1 Sol and
GPT-6 Luna on price. The announcement post ("Introducing GPT-6.1 Terra") returned 403 to
this run, so the facts here come from the API model page, the API changelog of
2026-10-26, and Simon Willison's write-up.

## Key points

- **Price per 1M tokens (2026-10-26):** $1 input, $0.05 cached input, $5 output. A
  30-minute cache write costs $1.25. That is half the price of GPT-6.1 Sol and about 10x
  GPT-6 Luna.
- **Limits:** 1,050,000-token context, at most 922,000 input tokens, and 128,000 output
  tokens. Knowledge cutoff is 2026-06-01, later than other GPT-6 models.
- **Reasoning effort:** none, low, medium (default), high, xhigh, max.
- **Tools in the Responses API:** web search, file search, image generation, code
  interpreter, hosted shell, apply patch, skills, computer use, MCP, and tool search.
- **Endpoints:** Responses, Chat Completions, and Batch. It is not available in the
  Realtime, Assistants, or fine-tuning APIs.
- **Quality, according to OpenAI:** OpenAI's Ted Sanders (on Hacker News, quoted by Simon
  Willison) said Terra is better than Astra and Sol at technical communication, about as
  good at vision and computer use but much faster, and somewhat weaker at most other
  tasks. No benchmark figures were checked for this note.
- **Availability:** in the API and in GitHub Copilot from 2026-10-26 (Pro, Pro+, Max,
  Business, Enterprise; billed at list price under usage-based billing, gradual rollout).

## Why it matters

Terra is a cheaper choice for computer-use and vision agents that do repetitive work,
where Astra or Sol cost too much and Luna is not capable enough. Its later knowledge
cutoff also helps.
