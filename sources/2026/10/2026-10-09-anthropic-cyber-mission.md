## Summary

Anthropic grouped its cyber-defence work under a "Cyber Mission" and launched two new
programmes: a Critical Infrastructure Defense Program for operators of physical systems,
and OSS Scanner, a free vulnerability-scanning service for open-source projects. It also
announced funding for open-source security foundations.

## Key points

- **Critical Infrastructure Defense Program (CIDP):** frontier Claude models, on-site
  engineers, and threat research for operational technology in power, water, transport,
  and government systems. Eleven founding partners: Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike,
  Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC, and
  Rockwell Automation.
- **OSS Scanner:** opt-in, free scanning of open-source projects with frontier models.
  Reports come with proof-of-concept exploits and suggested fixes; Anthropic expects a
  true-positive rate above 90%.
- **Funding:** support for the Python Software Foundation, Alpha-Omega, OpenSSF, and the
  Apache Software Foundation, plus Akrites and Gold Eagle for vulnerability coordination.
  A Defender Advantage Fund (0xDAF) pays for OSS Scanner.
- Builds on a June 2026 programme for US state and local governments, which Anthropic
  says now supports over half of US states.

## Why it matters

It extends the pattern seen in the Cyber Verification Program (2026-10-06): frontier
labs hold back offensive cyber capability by default and push it towards defenders.
Open-source maintainers can now get model-found vulnerability reports without paying,
which may raise the volume of security fixes, and of reports to triage.
