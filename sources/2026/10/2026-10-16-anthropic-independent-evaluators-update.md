## Summary

Anthropic has given a team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier models,
"employee-like access" to its systems: hardware, office space, and internal tools on par
with Anthropic's own risk teams. METR can publish its findings without Anthropic's
approval. The post also sets out a three-tier scheme for external evaluator access and
lists other partners at earlier stages.

## Key points

- Six METR staff have the access. They are walled off from the rest of METR by
  enforceable agreements.
- Anthropic cannot veto METR's publications. It can require redaction of three
  predefined categories only: customer data, privileged legal information, and model
  architecture details. METR may say publicly whether it thinks a redaction was
  justified.
- Access tiers:
  - **Tier 1:** narrow investigations; Anthropic chooses the access and must approve
    publication.
  - **Tier 2:** broader information and publication without approval, subject to the
    redaction categories.
  - **Tier 3:** employee-like access plus Tier 2 publication rights (METR today).
- SecureBio is reviewing the chemical and biological weapons chapter of the August 2026
  Risk Report, after publishing a pilot review of the February 2026 report.
- Accenture and Faculty are working towards a partnership on evaluation, red-teaming,
  alignment assessment, and safeguard testing. Hugging Face is in early talks.
- Stated criteria for evaluators: frontier expertise, independence (no equity or
  financial dependence), enough access, and freedom to publish. Anthropic compares the
  set-up to bank examiners working inside banks and says every frontier lab should do
  the same.
- The step follows Anthropic's signing of the White House Accord on Super Intelligence,
  which commits signatories to work with external auditors or evaluators.

## Why it matters

Most third-party model evaluation so far has been pre-release testing on terms set by
the lab. An evaluator with internal access and the right to publish without approval is
a stronger form of external oversight, and the tier scheme gives a vocabulary for
comparing what other labs offer.
