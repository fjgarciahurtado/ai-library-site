## Summary

Anthropic announced a revision of its Usage Policy, the rules for what Claude may be used
for across its products and API. The new version takes effect on 2026-11-12. Most changes
reword sections to match how the policy is already enforced; a few add or remove
restrictions.

## Key points

- **Deceptive activity:** a new consolidated section covers fake account networks, both
  state-sponsored and commercial, and efforts to "obscure who is behind a message or
  amplify content through fake accounts".
- **Elections:** the section is renamed "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes" and now
  targets voter deception and election disruption. The blanket ban on personalised
  campaign targeting is removed because it "covered legitimate civic work".
- **Weapons:** explicitly covers software and components that provide weapons
  functionality, including arming autonomous vehicles.
- **Surveillance and law enforcement:** clearer bans on tracking people without consent,
  and Claude may not decide "who to investigate, arrest, or charge".
- **High-risk use cases:** human-oversight requirements are unchanged, but the policy now
  lists which recommendations need human review, and adds hardware safety requirements
  for autonomous physical systems.
- **Abuse of models:** a new prohibition on sustained cruelty towards models in "extreme
  cases"; normal frustration and testing are excluded.
- **Supported regions:** clarifies enforcement against entities majority-owned from
  unsupported regions.

## Why it matters

Builders on Claude should check their use case against the new text before 2026-11-12.
Political campaign tooling becomes easier to build, while autonomous-hardware and
law-enforcement uses face more explicit limits. The clause on cruelty towards models is
an unusual step for a usage policy.
